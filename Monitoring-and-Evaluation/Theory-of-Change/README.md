# Theory of Change

## Overview

A Theory of Change explains how programme activities are expected to contribute to intended outcomes and longer-term impact.

It provides a logical basis for programme design, monitoring, evaluation, and learning.

---

## Example: Digital Financial Inclusion

### Problem

Underserved populations face barriers to accessing and effectively using formal and digital financial services.

### Inputs

- Funding
- Technical expertise
- Digital infrastructure
- Implementation partners

### Activities

- Financial literacy
- Digital skills training
- Agent support
- Community sensitisation

### Outputs

- Individuals trained
- Communities reached
- Agents supported
- Digital services promoted

### Short-Term Outcomes

- Increased knowledge
- Increased confidence using digital services
- Improved awareness of available financial products

### Medium-Term Outcomes

- Increased adoption
- Increased usage
- Greater access to financial services

### Long-Term Impact

Improved financial inclusion and economic resilience.

---

## Key Assumptions

- Digital services remain accessible.
- Users have adequate digital literacy.
- Service providers maintain reliable platforms.
- Communities are willing to adopt new services.
- Economic conditions do not significantly undermine adoption.

---

## Evaluation Questions

1. Did the intervention reach the intended beneficiaries?
2. Were planned activities implemented?
3. Did knowledge and awareness improve?
4. Did adoption of digital financial services increase?
5. Were there differences across demographic groups?
6. What factors influenced programme outcomes?
